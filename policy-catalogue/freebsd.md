## Verbatim excerpt

> "Do NOT submit a Pull Request for: […] Changes generated by AI tools without substantial human review and validation."

> "Automated accounts or chatbots must not submit pull requests or use pull request interactions to train AI or LLM systems."

> "Each commit must: […] Include one or more `Signed-off-by:` trailers certifying [Developer Certificate of Origin](https://developercertificate.org/)."

## Notes

**The only catalogued policy that protects the project as training data rather than only regulating AI as a source of contributions.** "…or use pull request interactions to train AI or LLM systems" governs what may be done *with* the project's contribution stream, not what may be submitted *into* it. Every other entry in the catalogue models AI in one direction — as a producer whose output arrives at the door. FreeBSD adds the opposite direction: the review conversation itself is an asset with terms attached. The v0.1 schema has no field, and no field shape, for this. It seems like a genuinely new axis rather than a variant of an existing one, and given how much public model training draws on open-source forges, probably not the last policy to state it.

**The AI clause is a routing rule, not a stance.** "Do NOT submit a Pull Request for… changes generated by AI tools without substantial human review" sits in a list whose other members are security changes, works in progress and controversial designs — none of which are prohibited by FreeBSD, only directed to a different channel (mailing lists, Phabricator). Read in place, the clause says *this pathway is the wrong one*, not *this contribution is unwelcome*. Every stance value in the schema (`disclosed-allowed`, `restricted`, `prohibited`, `unrestricted`) is a property of the contribution; FreeBSD's is a property of the channel. For a multi-forge project this distinction is load-bearing, and `restricted` plus a free-text note loses it.

**Human review is the qualifier, and it is the whole test.** The clause bars AI-generated changes *without substantial human review and validation* — so a reviewed AI-assisted change is not caught by it at all. Like OpenStreetMap and Mesa, the operative question is whether a human did the work of understanding, not whether a model was involved. Three projects, three phrasings, same test.

**DCO carries the provenance weight, as at QEMU — but to the opposite conclusion.** QEMU derives a full prohibition from the argument that DCO clauses (b) and (c) cannot be satisfied for model output. FreeBSD requires the same `Signed-off-by:` and reaches no such conclusion. If a `rationale` enum is added, DCO-provenance will not partition the corpus cleanly: the same instrument supports a ban in one project and coexists with permitted AI use in another. Worth recording as a caution rather than treating DCO as a proxy for stance.

**Both clauses are enforceable in the sense discussed in #22.** "Automated accounts must not submit pull requests" turns on account type, which is observable; the DCO trailer is a commit-metadata check. The AI-generated clause is not — "without substantial human review" is precisely the undecidable half. The policy contains one of each, in adjacent sections, which makes it a compact illustration of the split.
